# Medieval Chess Rules

This summary is extracted from the
[English rules for the Grunwald Tournament](https://drive.google.com/file/d/19PWjMzfwhXLvauD8rX6Iwe6u5n9HI2oU/view),
written down by Łysia & Zebra. The ruleset is used for the medieval chess
tournament held annually during the Battle of Grunwald reconstruction. The
event's official website also publishes an
[official Polish version](https://grunwald1410.pl/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Zasady-Grunwaldzkiego-Turnieju-Szachow-Sredniowiecznych-v1.pdf).

> **Translation note:** The Polish version contains a known translation mistake.
> If its wording differs from the English rules, consult the English source.

This document covers the chess rules only.

The source uses *italics* for the authors' interpretations of ambiguities in historical sources. The label **[Tournament-only]** below represents the source's double-asterisk marker for rules without historical source coverage.

## Board and initial position (page 1)

- Use an 8 × 8 board with contrasting light and dark squares.
- **a1 is light.**
- Arrange pieces in *mirror symmetry*, as in modern chess.
- The source diagram places White's back rank on a1-h1 and Black's on a8-h8, each in the order rook, knight, bishop, queen, king, bishop, knight, rook. White pawns occupy rank 2; Black pawns occupy rank 7.

## Piece movement (page 1)

### King

- Normally moves one square to any adjacent square.
- On its first move, may instead move two squares in any direction, including diagonally, or make a knight's move, *jumping over other pieces*.

### Queen

- Normally moves one square diagonally.
- On its first move, may also move two squares diagonally, two squares forward, or two squares sideways, *jumping over other pieces*.
- *It can never move to a square of the opposite colour*.
- Cannot move until its king has moved.

### Rook

- Moves any number of squares along a rank or file.
- *Cannot jump over other pieces.*

### Knight

- Moves in an L shape: one square forward/backward and two sideways, or two squares forward/backward and one sideways.
- Always lands on a square of the opposite colour.
- Can jump over other pieces.

### Bishop

- Moves **exactly two squares diagonally**, jumping over other pieces.

### Pawn

- Moves one square forward; on its first move, may move two squares forward.
- Cannot jump over other pieces.
- Captures one square diagonally forward.
- On reaching the last rank, is promoted to a piece with the queen's movement, *including the special first move, without needing to wait for the king's first move*.

## Game endings (pages 2-3)

| Situation | Definition and result |
| --- | --- |
| Naked mate | A player is left with only their king. Their opponent wins, *unless double naked mate is possible*. |
| Double naked mate **[Tournament-only]** | *The player left with only their king can capture the opponent's last remaining non-king piece, leaving only two kings. This is a draw.* |
| Checkmate | A player has no legal move and their king is under attack. Their opponent wins. |
| Stalemate | A player has no legal move and their king is not under attack. *The player unable to move loses, as in checkmate.* **[Tournament-only]** |
| Agreed draw | Players may agree to a draw at any point. |

## Rules that do not apply (page 2)

- **Castling as a single move:** absent. The source says a castling-like effect can be achieved in two moves.
- **Threefold repetition:** does not apply.
- **50-move rule:** does not apply.
- **En passant:** does not apply.

## Source ambiguities

These are extraction notes, not additional rules:

- **Double naked mate:** the PDF does not explicitly state how many moves the bare king has to capture the opponent's last piece. Do not infer a turn limit from this extraction.
- **Special first moves:** the PDF does not detail additional check, capture, or attacked-square restrictions for the king's special first move. Such details require interpretation rather than being supplied here as source rules.
